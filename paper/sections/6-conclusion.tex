\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \texttt{Rust2Isabelle}, an Isabelle/HOL backend for the Charon--Aeneas toolchain.
It combines Aeneas's functional translation with Isabelle-specific representations and semantic support.
Our evaluation demonstrates the feasibility of the approach and delineates its current coverage.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The prototype inherits Aeneas's restrictions on unsafe code and interior mutability.
Some built-ins, nested borrows, and complex control flow remain unsupported and machine-integer bounds and array lengths require well-formedness predicates rather than encoded directly in HOL types.
Loops and recursive definitions may introduce \texttt{axiomatization} or termination obligations. The backend is not verified by a semantics-preservation proof and does not yet generate functional-correctness specifications or proofs.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Future work will broaden the accepted Rust fragment by extending the \rprelude{} and completing support for missing types, library built-ins, and complex borrowing and control-flow patterns.
It will also reduce the current proof assumptions by generating termination proofs, handling mutually recursive definitions, and extending automatic well-formedness proofs.
Longer-term work includes proving semantics preservation for the backend and translating Rust specifications into Isabelle statements for functional-correctness verification.
Unsafe code and interior mutability would require a separate memory-aware extension to the current value-based representation.
